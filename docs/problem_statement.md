# Cross-Lingual Remote Workshop Facilitation

## Background

Hands-on workshops often require facilitators to observe group discussions, understand learners' progress, identify blockers, and provide timely guidance. This becomes significantly more difficult when facilitators and learners do not share a common language.

In remote or hybrid settings, audio and video conferencing allow participants to communicate, but they do not fully address the language and context barriers involved in interactive group activities.

## Target Users

The primary users are:

- Remote facilitators supporting hands-on workshops
- Local learner groups communicating in their preferred language
- Workshop organisers coordinating cross-lingual sessions

## Core Problem

Remote facilitators struggle to provide effective support when they cannot understand the language used in local group discussions.

Facilitators may be unable to determine:

- What the group is currently discussing
- What the group has already attempted
- Which problems or misunderstandings the group is facing
- What guidance has already been provided
- How the discussion relates to the current workshop activity

When a facilitator joins a group midway through a session, they must spend additional time asking learners to repeat or explain previous discussions before meaningful coaching can begin.

## Impact

This communication friction can result in:

- Delayed facilitator intervention
- Less time spent on meaningful coaching
- Repeated explanations and duplicated work
- Uneven support across learner groups
- Misunderstandings caused by inaccurate or incomplete translation
- Reduced learner participation and confidence
- Lower overall workshop effectiveness

## Limitations of Existing Solutions

Existing video-conferencing, captioning, and translation tools mainly focus on translating individual speech or messages.

They generally provide limited awareness of:

- Workshop objectives
- Current activities
- Group progress
- Previous discussions
- Decisions already made
- Questions or blockers raised by learners

As a result, facilitators may receive translated words without enough context to understand the group's actual situation.

## Project Scope

The project will focus on a hardware-agnostic software prototype that supports cross-lingual communication between remote facilitators and learner groups.

### In Scope

- Speech transcription
- Real-time or near-real-time translation
- Group discussion summaries
- Identification of progress and blockers
- Translated facilitator responses
- Basic workshop-context integration

### Out of Scope

- Specialised hardware
- Full video-conferencing functionality
- Automated grading
- Emotion or behaviour analysis
- Support for all languages
- Production-scale deployment

## Success Criteria

The prototype will be considered successful if it can demonstrate that:

- A facilitator can understand the main points of a learner-group discussion conducted in another language.
- A facilitator can quickly identify the group's current activity, progress, and blocker.
- Learners can receive facilitator guidance in their preferred language.
- The generated summary remains grounded in the group discussion and workshop context.
- The system reduces the amount of time required for a facilitator to understand a group's situation.
- The complete workflow can be demonstrated during a realistic workshop scenario.
